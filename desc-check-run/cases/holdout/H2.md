This PR gives `tabulate()` two new optional arguments, `footer` and `headergroups`. Each one defaults to `None`, and passing `None` yourself does exactly what leaving the argument out does. The existing arguments are where they were, in the same positions and with the same defaults.

I'll go through `footer` first and then `headergroups`.

`footer` supplies the cells for a row that appears underneath the table data. How that row is drawn depends on the output format. The `html` and `unsafehtml` output puts it in a `tfoot` that comes after the `tbody`, as a single row of `td` cells. For `pipe`, `github` and `asciidoc` it is simply one more data row. Every other format draws it the way it draws its header row, and that includes a `TableFormat` you construct yourself.

When a format has a line under its header, that same line is drawn a second time directly above the footer. `latex_longtable` repeats a bare `\hline` there, with no `\endhead`. In `rst` the repeated line is made of hyphens instead of `=` signs, and an empty first footer cell comes out as `..`.

On the input side, the footer can be any iterable of cells. Strings and bytes are rejected with a `TypeError`, and so is anything that isn't iterable. A footer that's shorter than the number of columns gets empty cells added on its left. That's the same padding headers already get, and it's what lets you leave the `showindex` column out of the footer. Give it more cells than there are columns and you get a `ValueError` instead.

With no rows and no headers, the footer alone decides how many columns there are. A `None` in the footer shows up as an empty cell. Footer cells never change what type a column is. In a numeric column, a footer number is formatted with `intfmt` when it is an integer and the column is an integer column as well; any other number in a numeric column goes through `floatfmt`. Numbers wrapped in ANSI colour codes follow the same two rules, and the colour codes are kept around the formatted value. Everything else, `True` and `False` included, is shown as text.

Footer cells are measured along with the rest of the table when we work out column widths. They are aligned the same way as the data, which means a footer can change how the data itself is aligned.

The other new argument, `headergroups`, describes column groups, either one level of them or several levels. Each group is written as `(title, span)` or `(title, span, align)`, using either a list or a tuple. Its span covers neighbouring columns, and the `showindex` column is counted among them. `align` takes `left`, `center` or `right`, and is `left` when you leave it out. Titles are passed through `str()`, and ANSI colour codes inside a title don't count toward its width.

To give one level, pass a list of groups such as `[("A", 2), ("B", 2)]`. To give several, pass a list of levels such as `[[("All", 4)], [("A", 2), ("B", 2)]]`. We only read the value as several levels when it is non-empty and every one of its items is itself a list or tuple made up only of lists or tuples. Anything else is read as a single level. A level is allowed to be empty, and an empty level behaves like one untitled group.

Spans in a level that add up to more columns than are available give a `ValueError`. When they fall short, we add an untitled group over the first columns. Group boundaries also have to nest: every boundary in one level must also be a boundary in the level below it.

For reference, these are the error cases.

- `headergroups` itself must be iterable and must not be a string or bytes; otherwise it raises `TypeError`.
- Each group has to be a list or tuple with two or three items, and `align` has to be a string. Breaking either rule is a `TypeError` too.
- A span must be a positive integer and must not be a boolean, and `align` must be one of `left`, `center` or `right`. Breaking either of these is a `ValueError`.
- `headergroups` is rejected with `ValueError` by the formats that don't support it: `asciidoc`, `github`, `jira`, `mediawiki`, `moinmoin`, `orgtbl`, `pipe`, `presto`, `textile`, `tsv`, and any `TableFormat` the caller builds.

The rest of this description goes through rendering, one family of formats at a time.

By merged-cell formats I mean `simple`, `plain`, `psql`, `pretty`, and all of the grid and outline formats. In these, every level is drawn above the normal header. Each title sits in a single merged cell that stretches across its group's columns and the separators between them, padded and aligned according to the group's `align`.

The top border breaks only at the top level's group boundaries. Even without headers, `simple` still draws a top line and a bottom line. Under a level we draw the format's between-rows line, or its below-the-header line for formats that have no between-rows line. Between two levels that line breaks at the lower level's boundaries, and under the last level it breaks at every column.

When a title plus its cell padding is wider than its merged cell, we widen the last column of that group. We widen the bottom level first. Apart from `rst`, no other format widens columns to fit titles.

`rst` follows the merged-cell layout for its level rows. Its top line is different, though: in `rst` it breaks at every column. Each level is followed by a line of hyphens that breaks at that level's own group boundaries. Titles in `rst` are always left-aligned, whatever `align` says, and an empty first title is written as `..`.

In `html` and `unsafehtml`, every level becomes a `tr` of `th` cells, and each cell carries its group's span and alignment. Those `th` cells also get a `scope="colgroup"` attribute. A group that covers only one column gets no `colspan` attribute. When there is a header row, the level rows go inside the `thead`. When there isn't, they get a `thead` of their own, placed before the `tbody`. `html` escapes titles; `unsafehtml` leaves them as they are.

For LaTeX, the level rows follow the opening lines. Cells are separated with `&` and each level row ends in ` \\`. A group spanning more than one column is written as `\multicolumn{SPAN}{A}{TITLE}`, with `A` being `l`, `c` or `r`, while a one-column group just gets its title. Escaping of titles matches the escaping of headers, with `latex_raw` as the exception. After any level that has titled groups we emit one rule per titled group: `\cmidrule(lr){FIRST-LAST}` in `latex_booktabs` and `\cline{FIRST-LAST}` in the other LaTeX formats. Column numbers in those rules start from 1.

Titles may include line breaks written as `\n`, `\r` or `\r\n`, with `\r\n` treated as one break. In the merged-cell formats, a level row is as tall as its tallest title, and shorter titles are filled out with blank lines at the bottom. Each line is aligned on its own, and the widest line sets the width the title needs. `rst` doesn't allow this: a title with more than one line raises `ValueError` there. `html` and the LaTeX formats keep the line breaks.

One last note on headers. When the table has rows, any headers beyond the longest row are dropped from the end.
